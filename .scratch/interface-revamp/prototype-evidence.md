## Decision state after the original comparison

The initial workbench's simplified actions may demonstrate placement of controls and how a form/status/confirmation feels. They do not establish authoritative domain validation, dependency-aware deletion, targeted command exclusion, real gesture cancellation, full navigation/edit separation, or restoration across variant remounts. Keeping the original maps does not automatically prove those newer contracts: the wrapper must integrate them, and the inherited renderer can still expose baseline behavior. Save/Retry are explicitly simulated; no storage failure/recovery result follows. A theme toggle in this snapshot is session presentation, not evidence of OS-derived or remembered theme preference. These limitations are acceptable for a first human comparison when clearly presented, and remain evidence gaps for final adoption/implementation.

The prototype instructions now explicitly state that the shared in-memory workspace, selection, tool, draft and theme survive a shell comparison, while each shell owns its own map viewport instance: **viewport state is not shared across variant remounts**. Theme remains session-only. Export uses applied in-memory map content, including after a simulated save error, and excludes unapplied inspector work; the actual downloaded files do not prove that every temporary decoration/gesture is cleaned under the full product contract. These are intentional/recorded prototype boundaries, not evidence of durable theme preference or complete view-memory preservation.

At that comparison handoff, the direction ticket remained `in-progress` and final adoption was blocked. The subsequent human reactions and final direction acceptance are recorded below. Prototype availability alone neither implements the product request nor closes it.

Useful reusable domain commands are `updateNativeFieldOptions`, `addCustomFieldDefinition`, `renameCustomFieldDefinition`, `updateCustomFieldOptions`, `updateCustomFieldApplicability`, `removeCustomFieldDefinition`, and `isCustomFieldApplicableToObject`. `FieldDefinitionsDialog` emits requests and owns draft/confirmation presentation; a prototype parent can validate and publish in memory without mounting `useLocalWorkspace` or writing IndexedDB. Its inherited open/discard policy must be adapted to the closed revamp contract: opening/closing the field panel preserves an unrelated inspector draft; conflicting field edits require explanation and Apply/Discard/Stay handling rather than silent loss. This reuse is an implementation recommendation, not final Nuxt UI adoption or permission to relax the preservation contract.

2026-10-14 clarification after inspecting the exact domain command: `updateCustomFieldApplicability` intentionally preserves every stored object value. Restricting applicability hides the field on excluded objects; restoring scope reveals their existing values. Only removal of select options and deletion of a field definition clear their affected values after explicit review. The revision must preserve this distinction.
